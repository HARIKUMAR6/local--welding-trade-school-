MIG / GMAW

MIG welding Gas Metal Arc

A continuous wire electrode feeds through a hand-held gun while shielding gas protects the weld. It is fast and steady, and it builds good habits for arc control.

01
Where it is used
Fabrication shops, production welding, auto-body and general repair, and structural assemblies.
Skills you build
  • Setting wire speed and voltage for the joint
  • Holding travel speed and gun angle
  • Running consistent, even beads
  • Welding fillet and groove joints
Ask about MIG
TIG welding

TIG / GTAW

TIG welding Gas Tungsten Arc

A tungsten electrode makes the arc while you add filler rod by hand, and shielding gas protects the weld. It is the slowest of the three processes and gives the most control.

  1. 01Coordinating torch and filler rod
  2. 02Controlling heat, often with a foot pedal
  3. 03Keeping a clean, tightly controlled bead
  4. 04Joining stainless and thin material

Where it is used Stainless steel and aluminum, thin material, piping, and welds where appearance and precision matter.

Ask about TIG

Stick / SMAW

Stick welding Shielded Metal Arc

An arc runs between a flux-coated electrode and the work. The flux burns to make shielding gas and slag, so no gas bottle is needed. The equipment is simple and rugged.

Where it is used
Structural steel, outdoor and field work, heavy equipment, and maintenance and repair.
Skills you build
  • Selecting the electrode and amperage
  • Holding arc length and drag angle
  • Removing slag between passes
  • Welding out of position
