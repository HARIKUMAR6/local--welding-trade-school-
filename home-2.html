01

MIG welding GMAW

Continuous wire and shielding gas make MIG the quickest process to build a steady hand on. It is widely used in fabrication and production work.

Core skills of the process

  • Machine setup: wire speed and voltage
  • Travel speed and gun angle
  • Consistent, even beads
  • Fillet and groove joints
MIG program details
02

TIG welding GTAW

A tungsten electrode makes the arc and you feed filler rod by hand. It is the slowest process and the most precise, used where clean, controlled welds matter.

Core skills of the process

  • Torch and filler-rod coordination
  • Amperage control with the foot pedal
  • Clean, tightly controlled beads
  • Stainless and thin-material joints
TIG program details
03

Stick welding SMAW

A flux-coated electrode makes its own shielding, so the process works outdoors and in rough conditions. It is a mainstay of structural and repair work.

Core skills of the process

  • Electrode selection and amperage
  • Arc length and drag angle
  • Slag removal between passes
  • Welding out of position

Practical lab training

A session at the bench.

Lab time follows the same rhythm every session: set up, weld, inspect, review, and log it.

  1. 01

    Suit up

    Helmet, gloves and protective clothing go on before the arc is struck.

  2. 02

    Set up

    Check the machine, gas and settings for the joint you are about to weld.

  3. 03

    Fit up

    Position and tack the metal so the joint is square and ready.

  4. 04

    Run the weld

    Strike the arc and run the bead with steady travel and control.

  5. 05

    Inspect and review

    Look over the weld, then go through it with your instructor.

  6. 06

    Log the hours

    Your lab time is recorded in the student portal.

Instructors who stand beside you.
