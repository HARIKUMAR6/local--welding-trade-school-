Teaching approach

Show it. Try it. Fix it. Repeat.

Every technique is taught through the same loop, so you always know what comes next and what to work on.

{{pic key="in-demo" alt="A welder in a hi-vis vest running a weld on a steel plate while another person guides the work from beside them" sizes="(min-width:1024px) 34vw, 100vw" pos="50% 55%"}} Guidance at the bench
  1. 01

    Demonstrate

    Your instructor shows the technique on real metal.

  2. 02

    Practice

    You run the same weld at your own booth.

  3. 03

    Observe

    Your instructor watches angle, speed and arc length while you work.

  4. 04

    Correct

    You get specific feedback, adjust, and go again.

Repeat until it is right

At the booth

What your instructor watches.

Small details make a sound weld. These are the fundamentals an instructor checks while you work, whatever the process.

{{pic key="in-watch" alt="Students in a school welding shop watching a classmate weld a steel frame, with red curtains and gas cylinders behind" sizes="(min-width:1024px) 55vw, 100vw" pos="40% 55%"}} Learning by watching
01Angle
The work and travel angle of the gun, torch or electrode.
02Speed
How fast you move along the joint, and how steady it stays.
03Arc length
The gap between the wire or electrode and the work.
04Bead
Its width and shape, and how cleanly it ties into the metal.

Safety

Safe habits come first.

Before an arc is struck, instructors make sure you are protected and your booth is ready.

  • A helmet with the right shade lens
  • Safety glasses under the helmet
  • Flame-resistant clothing and gloves
  • Good ventilation and a clear work area
